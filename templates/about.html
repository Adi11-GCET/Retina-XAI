About RETINA-XAI

Explainable AI for Diabetic Retinopathy Screening in Rural India — bridging the diagnostic access gap through transparent machine learning.

Prototype Limitations & Research Disclaimer
  • Educational & Research Prototype: This software is a college/hackathon demonstration prototype and is NOT a certified medical diagnostic device.
  • Demographic Generalizability: Public benchmark datasets (e.g. EyePACS, APTOS, IDRiD) reflect specific clinical cohorts and may not fully capture rural Indian demographic, nutritional, or mature cataract variations.
  • Fundus Hardware Variations: Image quality, illumination, and field-of-view vary widely across low-cost portable non-mydriatic cameras versus clinical tabletop systems.
  • Human-in-the-Loop Imperative: All automated predictions and Grad-CAM saliency maps MUST be evaluated by a qualified healthcare professional or ophthalmologist prior to any clinical decision.

What is Diabetic Retinopathy?

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is a microvascular complication of diabetes mellitus that damages the light-sensitive retinal tissue lining the back of the eye.

Chronically high blood glucose damages tiny retinal blood vessels, causing them to swell, leak fluid (exudates), hemorrhage, or become occluded. In advanced stages (proliferative DR), abnormal fragile new blood vessels proliferate on the retina, risking vitreous hemorrhage and tractional retinal detachment.

The 5 ICDR Severity Stages:
  1. 0 — No DR: No retinal abnormalities detectable.
  2. 1 — Mild DR: Microaneurysms only.
  3. 2 — Moderate DR: Microaneurysms, blot hemorrhages, hard exudates.
  4. 3 — Severe DR: >20 intraretinal hemorrhages in each of 4 quadrants, venous beading.
  5. 4 — Proliferative DR: Neovascularization, vitreous/preretinal hemorrhage.

Why Explainable AI (XAI)?

Deep learning models like Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) achieve high statistical accuracy on benchmark test sets. However, in medicine, a "black-box" prediction that outputs "Moderate DR (91%)" without context is inadequate for clinical trust and medico-legal accountability.

Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) computes the gradients of the target class score with respect to the final convolutional feature maps. This reveals the specific spatial regions—such as exudates or hemorrhages—that the network utilized to reach its classification.

Clinical Benefits of Grad-CAM:
  • Artifact Detection: Alerts clinicians if the AI erroneously focused on lens dust or eyelash reflections rather than true retinal lesions.
  • Decision Verification: Builds trust by confirming that the AI attention aligns with clinical ophthalmic features.
  • Training & Education: Assists rural vision technicians in learning to identify subtle early microaneurysms.

Reference Clinical Datasets
  • IDRiD (Indian Diabetic Retinopathy Image Dataset): Primary training cohort featuring expert ophthalmologist annotations for Indian rural clinics.
  • APTOS 2019 Blindness Detection: 3,662 fundus images captured under clinical conditions in rural and semi-urban Indian clinics (Aravind Eye Hospital).
  • EyePACS: 35,126 high-resolution images across diverse cameras from California diabetic screening programs.
  • Messidor & Messidor-2: Standardized European fundus dataset for validation and grading protocol comparison.
